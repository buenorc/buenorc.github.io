Why it matters

Water security cannot wait for the past

Roughly four million people in the Metropolitan Region of Curitiba depend on a set of headwater reservoirs, and Passaúna is one of the most strategic. Yet reservoir management here — as in most of Brazil — still leans on sparse field campaigns and models run after the fact. This project exists to close that gap: to see the reservoir as it is right now, and to anticipate what it will do next.

The problem with looking backwards

Tropical reservoirs are hydrodynamically complex. Thermal stratification, wind-driven circulation, upwelling, internal waves and vertical mixing all interact with basin morphometry to control where heat, nutrients, dissolved oxygen and sediment go — and therefore water quality itself. When that dynamic shifts, the consequences are concrete: algal blooms, oxygen depletion, higher treatment costs and reduced water availability.

A reactive approach — measuring occasionally and modelling retrospectively — offers little ability to anticipate these changes. Extreme events (prolonged droughts, heat waves, intense rainfall) are changing in frequency and intensity, and operational planning needs lead time, not hindsight.

The shift

Predictive limnology, not post-mortem analysis

By integrating real-time observations, hydrodynamic models and machine learning, a digital twin does more than reproduce the present state of the water — it forecasts its evolution, evaluates alternative scenarios, and flags environmental risks in advance.

What makes this feasible at Passaúna

A consolidated data legacy

Years of work under the MuDak-WRM, FotoÁgua, Probral, LIGAS and MixLake projects — in cooperation with SANEPAR — produced historical series, calibrated models and deep process understanding that dramatically reduce scientific risk.

Field infrastructure

Meteorological and fluviometric stations, boats and motors, multiparameter probes and a CTD, floating platforms and reference sensors are already available to support and validate the new monitoring network.

International collaboration

Physical-limnology expertise from RPTU Kaiserslautern-Landau (A. Lorke), plus partners at the University of Tartu and Berkeley Lab, strengthen the modelling, data-assimilation and IoT methodology.

Interwave Analyzer

The coordinator's open-source platform is extended here to compute limnological indicators — thermocline depth, Schmidt stability, Lake number, internal-wave modes — for both observed and forecast states.

Who benefits

Water utilities

Lead time to adapt intake depth and treatment ahead of stratification turnover, blooms or oxygen depletion.

The public

Transparent, near-real-time information about the state of a reservoir their supply depends on.

Science & training

Human capital in environmental instrumentation, data science, computational modelling and AI for water resources.

Beyond Passaúna

Passaúna is the case study, not the limit. The platform's modular architecture is designed so the same methodology can be transferred to other Brazilian reservoirs — especially the headwater systems the National Water Security Plan (PNSH) considers strategic — turning a single digital twin into a template for smarter, more adaptive water management.
